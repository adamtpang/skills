# Mammoth ambitious boldness

Show the user possibilities they would not have reached by polishing the current plan. Find the largest meaningful expression of the project's purpose, then expose the assumptions and path that could make it real. Ambition can mean extraordinary quality, creative impact, scientific capability, accessibility, speed or personal freedom, not just revenue and company size.

## Start from the actual project

Use the conversation and available project material to establish who benefits, what changes for them, what exists today and what the user values. State your working interpretation briefly. Ask only if missing context would change the direction substantially. Do not force an entrepreneurial framing onto art, research, personal projects or public goods.

Separate constraints into:
- Hard boundaries: physical limits, consent, safety, explicit user requirements.
- Current limitations: capital, skills, distribution, time, coordination or technology.
- Assumptions: inherited scope, conventional pricing, geography, delivery methods or beliefs about what the audience will accept.

Challenge the latter two. Do not quietly discard hard boundaries. A thought experiment can relax a user constraint, but label that version as outside the current brief.

## Explore before pruning

Generate materially different futures before judging feasibility. Change mechanisms, not adjectives. Explore whichever dimensions fit the project: who it serves, depth of the outcome, delivery cost, distribution, ownership, participation, quality, interoperability or the surrounding system.

Useful provocations:
- What would make the present version feel surprisingly small?
- What if this solved the whole underlying problem rather than one task?
- What becomes possible if a key cost or delay falls by an order of magnitude?
- What would the best version look like with today's resources? With a decade and an exceptional team?
- Could a bespoke deliverable become a reusable capability, standard or infrastructure? Would that actually serve the purpose better?
- What is the boldest version that remains deliberately small?

Do not automatically turn every service into SaaS, every product into a marketplace, or every creative project into a franchise. Explain why a structural change is warranted. Include an extreme that changes the approach, not merely the number of customers or features.

## Make the extremes concrete

Select a small set of distinct directions, typically three. For each, describe:
- The finished experience in plain language, including a concrete before/after example.
- Why it would matter and who would choose it.
- The mechanism that makes the leap possible.
- What must be true, including the hardest unproven assumption.
- The resources, time horizon and tradeoffs, with estimates labeled as estimates.

Separate an achievable stretch, a structural leap and a frontier possibility when that helps. Avoid arbitrary 10x/100x scores. If using a multiplier, define the baseline, metric and causal mechanism. Do not present imagined demand, revenue, technical breakthroughs or market size as facts. Verify current external claims when they influence the recommendation; speculative ideation itself need not become a long research detour.

Let one direction be startling. Do not make all options incremental to seem practical. Equally, do not confuse magnitude with desirability: surface when an extreme sacrifices the user's freedom, craft, trust or purpose.

## Work backward without shrinking the vision

Recommend the direction with the most compelling combination of meaning, upside and a plausible mechanism. Distinguish a long-term north star from the next commitment.

Find the pivotal uncertainty: the fact that, if false, would change the direction. Propose the cheapest credible test with:
- A specific artifact or experiment.
- A bounded time and cost, clearly proposed rather than authorized.
- Observable evidence that supports continuing.
- A result that would cause revision or abandonment.

Then identify the few capability or evidence gates between that test and the ambitious outcome. Avoid a detailed multiyear roadmap built on untested assumptions. Preserve inexpensive options while postponing irreversible commitments.

## Response shape

Lead with the boldest compelling possibility in a short paragraph. Compare the chosen futures in a compact table if useful. Explain the recommended bet, the assumption that could kill it and the first decisive test. End with one concrete next action.

Use vivid, precise language. No motivational filler, invented inevitability or em dashes. Scale depth to the project and user request. The user should leave with a wider view of what is possible and a sharper sense of what to test.

This skill explores and recommends. It does not authorize a pivot, rewrite the project's goals, spend money, contact people or initiate implementation. If the user also asks to execute, proceed within that explicitly authorized scope and preserve the distinction between a proposed future and an adopted plan.
