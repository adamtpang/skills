# Productize

Produce an evidence-backed offer and the smallest useful proof of delivery. Completing this brief does not mean the business is validated.

## Establish current truth

Read project instructions, current handoff, pending work, offer, evidence, and relevant implementation. Preserve concurrent work. Identify whether the target is a company, one product within a company, a service, media, or an internal tool. Honor authoritative classifications; do not commercialize an internal tool just to fill a template.

Compare documented promises with customer-facing copy, checkout configuration, and delivery behavior. Date sources. Recent receipts and code support implementation claims, but do not establish today's deployed state. Surface contradictory prices, stale documents, mocks, and missing dependencies. Missing evidence is unknown, not zero.

## Define the sellable unit

Choose one flagship offer within the user's target. Separate the customer outcome from the machinery. Specify:

- Buyer, the situation that makes the problem urgent, and where that buyer already looks, works, or spends.
- Exact deliverable, format, inputs, eligibility, quantity limits, turnaround, and remaining customer effort.
- Acceptance criteria, exceptions, revisions, and failed-delivery handling. Mark proposed commercial terms as drafts.
- Existing alternatives, including manual work, free tools, and doing nothing. Treat the proposed advantage as a hypothesis until demonstrated.

Use a plain offer sentence: “For [buyer facing situation], [product] delivers [concrete result] from [inputs], within [bounded scope].” Do not promise revenue, rankings, or outcomes the product cannot control.

## Economics and access

Preserve approved prices; distinguish observed prices, proposed experiments, and unresolved contradictions. Browse current primary sources when competitor claims or current rates affect the decision. Advertised prices prove competing offers exist, not sales volume.

Apply abundance pricing: substantial customer value, broad useful access, and enough contribution to sustain delivery and improvement. Consider free samples and usage tiers when useful. Include labor, review, retries, support, acquisition, fees, and infrastructure, not just model tokens. Label assumptions and show sensitivity when costs are unknown. Local subscriptions do not make time or capacity unlimited.

## Bounded proof

Identify one binding constraint. Reuse existing evidence and relevant tests. Complete one reversible local demonstration, sample, verification, or offer artifact addressing it. Label synthetic or owned/permitted inputs; synthetic success is not customer validation. Do not bypass product eligibility guards to manufacture a demo.

Define the next demand experiment: qualified audience, exact offer, behavioral outcome, denominator, cost/time bound, and continue/change/stop criteria. Prefer use, payment, successful delivery, and repeat use over compliments or clicks. Small-sample thresholds are decision rules, not statistical certainty.

Skill invocation does not authorize messages, publishing, purchases, price changes, deployment, or new business commitments. Complete already-authorized local work and leave dependent external actions with their owner. Do not create tasks or recurring work unless requested.

## Deliver

Save a concise PRODUCTIZE.md or follow the project's artifact convention. Include the offer, facts versus hypotheses, scope and acceptance, economics, demand experiment, one blocker, verification receipts, and next human action. Link sources and record what actually ran. Update the existing handoff with a short pointer when required; do not silently rewrite strategy or offer documents.

Report separately what changed, what local proof established, what remains unverified, and the next action. A generated plan, passing unit tests, or payment link is not proof of customer demand.
