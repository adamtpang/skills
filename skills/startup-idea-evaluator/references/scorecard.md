# Scorecard

Score each category from 0 to 5. Multiply by its weight divided by five. Do not turn unknowns into average scores; mark them unknown and provide a provisional range.

| Dimension | Weight | 0 | 3 | 5 |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| People affected | 15 | Tiny or undefined audience | Clear segment with recurring exposure | Large population or a concentrated, high-leverage system |
| Problem quality | 15 | Preference or invented pain | Repeated, costly pain with an inferior workaround | Severe, urgent, neglected problem with durable demand |
| Market growth | 10 | Contracting or structurally capped | Stable market with a credible growth driver | Fast-growing market or expanding category with cited catalyst |
| Willingness to pay | 15 | No buyer or budget | Plausible buyer and comparable spend | Named buyer, existing budget/replacement spend, and strong pricing proof |
| Newness / frontier | 10 | Commodity clone with no wedge | Differentiated execution in a known category | Newly possible category or contrarian frontier with a defensible wedge |
| Utility delta | 15 | No meaningful improvement | Material improvement on one important metric | Order-of-magnitude or category-changing improvement, measurable against status quo |
| Why now | 10 | No changed condition | One plausible catalyst | Multiple concrete changes make the idea newly feasible, with timing evidence |
| Founder-problem fit | 10 | No earned connection | Relevant skill or access | Lived pain, proprietary access, or deep domain work that creates non-obvious insight |

## Interpretation

- **80–100: Pursue**, clear good quest and unusually strong founder fit. Build or raise a tightly scoped first wedge.
- **65–79: Validate**, attractive thesis, but one or two expensive assumptions need evidence before commitment.
- **45–64: Narrow**, problem or market may be real; the proposed solution, buyer, or founder fit is weak.
- **0–44: Pass**, insufficient impact, utility delta, timing, willingness to pay, or fit.

## Evidence standards

- Separate social impact from market value. Both matter, but neither proves the other.
- Treat total-addressable-market slides as claims, not evidence. Prefer customer budgets, contracts, pricing, replacement spend, and observed behavior.
- Define the state of the art before claiming a delta. Compare cost, time, accuracy, access, reliability, or outcome.
- "New" means newly feasible or newly differentiated, not merely new to the founder.
- Require a counterfactual: what happens if this company does not exist?
