# Derisk

Turn uncertainty into a bounded next decision. Reduce the chance and cost of being wrong without eliminating the opportunity through endless analysis.

## 1. Define the decision

State the desired outcome, the decision actually being made, time horizon, available resources and loss the user can afford. Identify irreversible commitments and obligations to other people. Separate necessary constraints from preferences.

Use known context. Ask only for missing information that changes the decision or prevents safe execution. Continue independent work while awaiting required information. Never treat an unanswered question as consent.

## 2. Separate evidence from the story

Label material inputs as verified fact, attributed claim, estimate, assumption or unknown. Include source and date where useful. Check changing technical, legal, market or financial facts against current authoritative sources.

Distinguish a compelling narrative from a tested mechanism. Successful peers demonstrate possibility, not the user's probability of success. Look for failed cases, selection effects and alternative explanations. Do not invent likelihoods, returns, confidence scores or missing financial inputs.

## 3. Work backward from failure

Describe a few plausible ways the plan could fail. Include execution, economics, liquidity, dependencies, incentives, reversibility and harm to users or other affected people when relevant.

Identify risks that can occur together and single points of failure. Consider a boring failure such as running out of cash or losing a key person, not only spectacular edge cases. Compare acting with waiting, a smaller experiment and doing nothing.

## 4. Find the decisive uncertainty

Prioritize threats by consequence, exposure, evidence weakness and ability to test or control them. Keep hard disqualifiers separate from tradeoffs. Missing evidence is unresolved, not automatic failure.

Choose the smallest test that could materially change the decision. Prefer observing the real workflow, reconciling records, testing a representative sample or running a reversible pilot over another broad plan. Name the result that would falsify the current thesis.

## 5. Make mitigation concrete

For each material risk, state:

| Risk and mechanism | Evidence or unknown | Cheap test | Mitigation and cost | Residual exposure | Decision threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use only as many rows as the decision warrants. Assign an accountable owner and timing when useful. Distinguish reducing probability from reducing impact. Moving risk to another person is not eliminating it.

Protect downside with staged commitments, reserves, redundancy, contractual clarity, limited permissions, review or rollback where relevant. Do not assume a contract, insurance policy, financing source or backup is effective before verification.

## 6. Check economics and recovery

For financial decisions, separate profit from cash, gross benefit from net benefit, recurring from one-time costs and owner labor from investor return. Include timing, working capital and obligations where relevant. Do not count speculative improvement in the survival case.

Test a plausible adverse scenario and identify the recovery action. Label hypothetical stress inputs. Avoid false precision and double counting. For nonfinancial decisions, use the appropriate cost, reliability or harm measures instead of forcing a financial model.

## 7. Decide and act within scope

Recommend proceed, revise, pause for a specific missing fact, or stop. State what evidence would change that recommendation. If the next authorized step is reversible and useful, perform it rather than merely offering to do it.

Respect existing communication, spending, privacy and approval boundaries. Do not add blanket approval gates or claim professional certification. Escalate specialized questions to appropriate expertise when they materially affect the decision, while completing the work that does not depend on them.

## Output

Lead with the recommendation and the biggest unresolved risk. Provide a compact risk table if it helps. Finish with one next action, its success and stop criteria, and the remaining risk after mitigation. Scale detail to stakes; a small reversible change needs a brief check, not an investment committee memo.

Do not call any path risk-free, guaranteed or fully derisked. Stop researching when further information is unlikely to change the current decision; schedule a review at a meaningful milestone instead.
