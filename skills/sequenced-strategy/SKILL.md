# Sequenced Strategy: Let Each Rung Fund The Next

Build a sequence where every stage is viable, produces evidence, and unlocks a
specific capability needed by the next stage.

## Method

1. State the long-term outcome and the constraints that prevent starting there:
   capital, credibility, production capacity, technology, distribution, or
   customer trust.
2. Identify a known market with current buyers, budget, and an urgent problem.
3. Design the first rung as high-value, low-volume, and manually deliverable.
   It should maximize learning and margin without requiring future scale.
4. Define what the rung must produce besides cash: proof, reusable technology,
   delivery capability, distribution, data, or customer trust.
5. Specify the gate for advancing. Future ambition does not excuse a weak first
   rung.
6. Design later rungs that use accumulated capability to lower cost, increase
   volume, deepen value, or approach the larger mission.
7. State how much cash is retained, reinvested, or extracted at each rung. Use
   ranges when pricing evidence is incomplete.
8. Name the stop condition if a rung fails to prove its assumptions.

## Output

| Rung | Buyer | Offer | Price logic | Volume | Proof created | Capability funded | Advance gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |

Then state:

- **Entry wedge:** why this buyer pays now.
- **Compounding assets:** what survives each delivery.
- **Cash sequence:** how the business funds progress without fantasy revenue.
- **Stop rule:** evidence that prevents advancing.
- **Next rung action:** the smallest real test of rung one.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent exact prices or demand without evidence.
- Each rung must work economically on its own. A speculative future stage cannot
  subsidize a broken present offer.
- Premium means high value and constrained volume, not arbitrary markup.
- Protect personal runway. A company sequence does not authorize an all-in
  personal financial bet.
