# Tier List

Turn the options already in the conversation into a useful decision. Rank for the user's current purpose, not generic prestige. This skill works in any chat and has no required project, account, or private data dependency.

## Establish the comparison

- Resolve phrases such as "these" or "that site" from the current conversation. Use the supplied options; do not silently replace them with a new shortlist. If the set is too large or inaccessible to cover, state exactly what was reviewed and what remains unranked.
- Infer the desired outcome, preferences, budget, timing, and non-negotiables from the latest context. State the few criteria that drive the ranking. Ask one concise question only when a missing fact could materially reverse the recommendation; otherwise proceed with labeled assumptions.
- Separate hard eligibility constraints from preferences. Unavailable, unaffordable, or incompatible options can have high theoretical fit but must be marked blocked or ineligible for the current decision. Do not recommend them as ready to act on.

## Ground the tiers

- Compare on consistent criteria. Use current primary sources for changeable facts such as prices, availability, requirements, and schedules when tools permit. Cite the actual supporting pages. For subjective preferences, explain the judgment without dressing it up as research.
- Distinguish verified facts, the user's reports, inference, and unknowns. Missing evidence means "unranked / needs verification", not automatically F. A directory profile does not establish actual availability, effectiveness, or personal compatibility.
- Avoid arbitrary decimal scores. If numerical weights help or are requested, show their rationale and keep hard constraints outside the weighted score. Allow ties and empty tiers; do not force a distribution.
- When comparing people, rank relevant professional fit and publicly supported experience, not human worth. Do not infer character, private traits, or protected attributes from a profile.

## Tier meanings

- S: Exceptional fit for this goal with a clear advantage over the alternatives.
- A: Strong fit with manageable tradeoffs.
- B: Viable, but has a meaningful compromise or a narrower use case.
- C: Weak fit for the present goal; worthwhile mainly if circumstances change.
- D: Poor fit with major disadvantages.
- F: Fails a stated non-negotiable or is clearly unsuitable on available evidence. Explain the failure, not an insult.
- Unranked: Evidence is insufficient to judge responsibly.

These labels describe fit. Show availability or eligibility separately so an excellent but unavailable option cannot displace an actionable choice without explanation.

## Deliver the result

Lead with the best actionable choice and why. Default to a compact table sorted from strongest to weakest: Tier | Option (source link when available) | Why it fits | Main tradeoff or status. For many options, expand the strongest tiers and group lower tiers with brief, specific reasons, while preserving coverage of the requested set.

Finish with one concrete next step. If there is no defensible winner, identify the smallest check or trial that would resolve the decision. State what would change the ranking when uncertainty matters. Honor a request for TLDR and avoid turning a ranking into a new planning project.

Ranking does not authorize purchases, applications, bookings, messages, scheduling, or other external changes. Keep private chat context out of public searches and artifacts. Follow the active chat's communication and approval rules.
