# Primary sources and adaptation

Checked 2026-09-07. These are Lulie Tanett's philosophical accounts, not established universal explanations of motivation. The skill's questions, experiments, privacy rules, and stopping conditions are our practical adaptation.

- [Discipline is fighting yourself](https://www.lulie.co.uk/discipline/): Lulie argues for resolving conflicting wants through reasoning instead of forcing compliance. Use this as an invitation to understand an objection, without promising that every hard task will become effortless.
- [Spark Joy = HELL YEAH or no = Fun criterion](https://www.lulie.co.uk/fyeahjoy/): She attributes the fun criterion to David Deutsch and connects it with Marie Kondo and Derek Sivers. Her explanation concerns alignment among ideas that may not yet be articulated. The skill translates this into checking willingness and remaining objections, rather than demanding constant excitement.
- [Coercion can't make you think particular things](https://www.lulie.co.uk/coercion-cant-make-you-think/): She emphasizes creativity, experimentation, and an attractive direction beyond merely escaping what feels bad. This informs the question about a version the user would enjoy exploring.
- [Why people get stuck](https://www.lulie.co.uk/coercivescripts/): Her suggested response includes compassion, considering what one wants to be doing, and noticing patterns without judgment. Borrow that stance without diagnosing the user as governed by the scripts she describes.

When explaining the method, cite the relevant essay and distinguish her claims from our interpretation. Do not fabricate quotations, credentials, endorsements, or a named clinical treatment. Routine use can rely on these notes; research again when asked about a new or precise claim.
