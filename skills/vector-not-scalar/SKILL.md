# Vector Not Scalar: Combine Speed With Direction

High activity is a scalar. Progress also needs direction. Diagnose whether each
major effort advances, misses, or opposes the actual destination.

## Method

1. Define the destination as one observable outcome and time horizon.
2. List the major current efforts with hours, money, attention, and expected
   result.
3. Classify direction: aligned, neutral, uncertain, or opposed.
4. Estimate outcome contribution and feedback latency. Do not reward effort for
   its own sake.
5. Identify the fastest misaligned effort. It usually creates the most waste.
6. Stop, redirect, or reduce neutral and opposed work. Preserve necessary
   maintenance explicitly rather than pretending it is progress.
7. Concentrate capacity on the aligned effort with the fastest reliable feedback
   loop.
8. Define a course-correction trigger before the next review.

## Output

| Effort | Resources | Direction | Expected contribution | Feedback latency | Verdict |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Then state:

- **Destination:** the outcome all vectors are judged against.
- **Net direction:** whether the current portfolio points toward it.
- **Largest counter-vector:** the effort consuming the most resources while
  missing the outcome.
- **Correction:** what stops, moves, or receives more capacity.
- **Next measurement:** evidence that confirms the new direction.

## Boundaries

- Do not glorify extreme hours, sleep loss, or impossible schedules.
- Necessary maintenance, health, and relationships are not automatically noise.
  Label them accurately and budget them.
- Course correction is expected. Do not preserve a bad direction because of
  sunk cost.
- If the destination itself is unclear, establish it before ranking effort.
