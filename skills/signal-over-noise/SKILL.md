# Signal Over Noise: Fund What Changes The Outcome

Judge work by whether it causally improves the product or service, delivery,
customer access, measured learning, cash collection, reliability, or safety.

## Method

1. Define the current outcome and primary metric.
2. Inventory material uses of money, time, and attention.
3. Classify each item:
   - **Signal:** directly improves the outcome or produces decisive evidence.
   - **Necessary overhead:** required for safety, law, reliability, or continued
     operation but not itself progress.
   - **Unproven:** plausible contribution with no causal evidence yet.
   - **Noise:** no defensible path to the current outcome.
4. Require an owner, mechanism, metric, and review date for every signal or
   unproven item.
5. Stop noise, cap unproven work, budget overhead honestly, and move resources
   toward the strongest measured signal.
6. Define a cheap test for the highest-cost unproven item.

## Output

| Item | Cost | Class | Causal mechanism | Evidence | Owner | Decision |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Then state:

- **Primary signal:** the work most likely to move the metric.
- **Largest noise source:** the easiest meaningful resource recovery.
- **Necessary overhead:** protected but not mislabeled as progress.
- **Unproven budget:** capped amount and validation deadline.
- **Reallocation:** where recovered resources go now.

## Boundaries

- Distribution and sales can be signal. Do not imitate a famous company that
  avoided advertising when the current business lacks customer access.
- Brand work can be signal when measured trust or conversion is the constraint.
- Do not delete compliance, security, accessibility, maintenance, or health as
  noise merely because their benefit is preventative.
- Correlation, visibility, and busyness are not causal evidence.
