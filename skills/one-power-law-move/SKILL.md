# One Power-Law Move

Find one concrete action with a plausible chance of producing a disproportionately large improvement in the user's actual outcome. Treat "power law" as a leverage heuristic, not a claim that the situation follows a measured statistical distribution.

## Find the Move

- Read the current situation and relevant available context. Identify the user's desired outcome, current bottleneck, previous attempts, and binding constraints. Separate the outcome from the user's proposed tactic: restoring a particular account may serve a larger goal of finding work.
- Ask one concise question only if the missing answer could change the winner. Otherwise state the consequential assumption and proceed.
- Consider a small set of materially different actions. Include removing a dependency, changing the decision-maker or channel, fixing the root cause, and building a reusable asset where relevant. Do not default to escalation, networking, automation, or more effort regardless of context.
- Compare likely outcome improvement, feasibility, time to impact, effort, reversibility, and downside. Look for actions that unlock many downstream steps, reach a binding decision-maker, compound, or eliminate repeated work. Use qualitative judgments unless grounded quantitative inputs exist; do not invent probabilities, multipliers, or precision.
- Verify current facts that determine feasibility through appropriate sources or tools. Distinguish official requirements, first-person anecdotes, and inference. A vivid success story is not a success rate.
- Stress-test the leading action: what must be true for it to work, what could make it harmful, and does a simpler alternative dominate? Choose one executable action, not an abstract aspiration, a bundle disguised as one step, or an exhaustive checklist.

## Deliver and Execute

Keep the result short. Include:

1. **The move:** a specific verb, object, and destination or owner.
2. **Why this one:** the bottleneck it changes and why it outranks the strongest alternative.
3. **Do it now:** the smallest usable first step, such as a command, artifact, experiment, or copy-ready draft.
4. **Checkpoint:** observable evidence of progress and a bounded time or effort limit, after which to reassess. Label a chosen review date as a planning choice, not a promised response time.

State the most important uncertainty or downside. If no action has credible outsized leverage, say so and choose the best next experiment or information-gathering step instead of manufacturing a breakthrough.

"Run this skill" authorizes the prioritization workflow, not new external commitments or communication. Execute the selected action only within existing authorization and safety constraints. Otherwise prepare its first step and state what the user must do. Do not silently start recurring monitoring.

Preserve essential safety and maintenance work even when it is not the highest-leverage action. Do not imply that one bet guarantees the entire outcome or replaces every necessary step.
