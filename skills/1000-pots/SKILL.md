# 1000 pots

Make small things, finish them, learn by selecting the best, and make the next batch. Treat 1000 as a direction for sustained practice, not an instruction to generate 1000 items in one run.

Read the project's instructions and current work. Identify its smallest meaningful finished artifact: an essay, sketch, working feature, demo, experiment, or other useful unit. Preserve the user's taste and existing design. Do not replace the project with a productivity dashboard.

Define a short creation loop and a separate curation loop. A useful default is one artifact each working session and a review after seven completed artifacts; user cadence overrides this. Keep one artifact active. Make the unit smaller when time is short. Do not let one draft absorb every future session in pursuit of perfection.

For creation, maintain a shelf of concrete seeds with provenance. Pick one, explore a few promising angles, make a rough version, refine its central idea, and complete it. AI can offer a bullet scaffold, ask a useful question, suggest variations, and help revise. Keep the author's intended meaning and distinguish suggestions from their original material. Never silently overwrite their work.

For curation, compare finished artifacts, keep the strongest, name one specific lesson, and use that lesson in the next batch. Archive or defer weaker ideas reversibly. Count completed artifacts separately from published or delivered artifacts; do not call generated suggestions finished work.

Quantity is a practice strategy, not permission to fabricate evidence, expose private material, produce unsafe work, or publish without authorization. Keep a small quality floor appropriate to the domain: coherent, faithful, usable, and checked where errors matter. Do not assert historical anecdotes about artists as fact without verification.

Run the skill by making the requested project change or completing a concrete small artifact, not merely reciting the philosophy. Set a bounded scope based on the user's request. Record the artifact unit, cadence, completion criterion, curation interval, and next creation step in a compact project note. If implementing a shelf or workbench, use existing storage/auth/design conventions, label manual imports honestly, retain provenance, and support revision without data loss.

Close with what was made, what was checked, and the next small creation action. Do not enable schedules, mass generation, or external distribution merely because the user asked for prolific practice.
